## The problem Embed solves

Organisations can't see whether their people can actually do what matters, and what people learn fades without use.

- **Work is changing fast.** AI is likely to change every part of work. People still play an essential role, but what they need to know and do keeps shifting, and capability has to keep up.
- **Knowing fades.** People learn something once, then rarely use it until the moment it counts. Without use, skills and knowledge decay. One 2025 review of 457 reports found half the accuracy gained in training was lost after about six and a half months.
- **Completion isn't capability.** A completion record shows someone sat a course. It doesn't show they can apply it on a busy Tuesday.
- **Leaders are flying blind.** Capability gaps stay invisible until a mistake, an incident, an audit finding or a lost deal exposes them.
- **More training isn't the fix.** People are already short of time. The answer is keeping what matters alive, not adding more courses.

**Frame it for leaders, not trainers.** The problem is performance and risk, not learning. Lead with what it costs the organisation, not with how training works.

## The product

Embed does two jobs: it keeps what matters fresh in people's heads, and it proves who can do it.

### 1. Keep it fresh (reinforcement)

- **Brain nudges:** short, spaced practice, no more than 3 minutes a week, that brings knowledge back before it fades.
- **Scenarios:** realistic situations built from your own policies and procedures. They test judgement, not memory.
- **Adaptive feedback:** feedback on every answer. Each person gets more practice where they're slipping and less where they're solid.

### 2. Prove it (assurance)

- **Capability state:** what each person can do today, in four measures: confidence, evidence strength, freshness and risk.
- **Drift and risk:** shows which skills are fading, and for whom, before it shows up as a mistake.
- **Admin dashboard and analytics:** a live view across every team, with the skills that need attention at the top. Capability over time, next to the business measures you already track.
- **Evidence trail:** dated evidence behind every capability state, so you can show people are competent, not just trained.

### 3. Built from what you have, led by people

- **AI content builder:** drafts capabilities, questions and scenarios from a PDF, PowerPoint, some text or your own words.
- **Human review:** a person approves, edits or rejects every AI-drafted question before anyone sees it.
- **Responsible AI:** human-led, grounded in your own content, and explainable.

### Getting started, in about 20 minutes

1. **Add users.** Upload a CSV or add them by hand. It can be just you to begin with.
2. **Generate content.** From a PDF, PowerPoint, text or your own words. Capabilities and questions are created as part of the content set.
3. **Assign it.** Give a content set to a group.
4. **Go live.** People start practising and the picture of capability starts to build.

**Where it sits:** alongside a learning management system, not instead of one. The LMS records that training happened. Embed shows whether it stuck, and keeps it that way.

## Proof: the science agents may cite

Embed is built on two of the best-evidenced findings in learning science: spacing practice over time, and recalling rather than re-reading. Use these claims word for word, always with the source. They are the same claims as the science page, checked on 16 Nov 2026.

### Headline proof (randomised controlled trial)

**40% fewer inappropriate screening tests in the 72 weeks after the programme ended.** 95 clinicians were split into two groups. One got short clinical scenarios by email, each with immediate feedback, spaced over 36 weeks. The other got nothing. Afterwards, inappropriate screening was 7.8% in the group that practised and 13.1% in the group that didn't. Source: Kerfoot and colleagues, 2010.

When you use it, say it was clinicians in a trial, not Embed customers.

|---|---|

| The original forgetting curve, measured by Hermann Ebbinghaus in 1885, was replicated in 2015. | Murre & Dros, 2015 |
| A review of 839 assessments from 317 experiments found the best gap between practice sessions grows with how long the memory needs to last. | Cepeda and colleagues, 2006 |

### Rules for evidence

- This research is about the methods Embed uses. It is not a study of Embed. Never write "Embed is proven to…" or "Embed improves X by 40%".
- Don't claim Embed reaches one-to-one tutoring results. Bloom's "2 sigma" finding is a question he asked, not something Embed delivers.
- Say spacing is "timed to how long it needs to last", not "widening gaps".
- There are no Embed customer results or testimonials yet. Don't write any.

### How Embed handles personal data (Learnt.ai's own statement)

- **Roles:** under UK GDPR, the customer is normally the Data Controller for its learner and employee data. Learnt.ai is the Data Processor, and processes personal data only to provide, secure, support and improve Embed, following the customer's documented instructions.
- **What's processed:** names, business email addresses, role and team, learning assignments, answers, capability-confidence information and audit records. Embed doesn't need patient data or other special-category data to work.
- **What the AI sees:** learning materials, capability descriptions and existing learning content. Not learner names, email addresses, individual answers or confidence scores.
- **Where processing happens:** hosting is in the US, and AI processing may take place outside the UK or EEA unless regional processing has been set up. Transfers are covered by an approved safeguard, such as the UK International Data Transfer Agreement or the UK Addendum to the EU Standard Contractual Clauses.
- **Protection:** role-based access, tenant-level privacy rules, encryption in transit and at rest, audit records, controlled admin access and supplier due diligence.
- **Subprocessors:** a small number, for hosting, AI processing, file processing and transactional email.
- **Your rights:** Learnt.ai supports data-subject requests, security incidents, data export and deletion. Data is returned or deleted after the contract ends, subject to legal, security and backup-retention needs.

## Voice and style

Write like a confident, plain-speaking expert talking to a busy leader: short, specific and honest.

- **British English.** Organisation, programme, colour, licence (noun), practise (verb).
- **Short, punchy lines.** One idea per sentence. Cut anything that doesn't earn its place.
- **Plain words.** "Use" not "leverage", "help" not "empower", "people" not "human capital".
- **Specific beats impressive.** "3 minutes a week" beats "minimal time commitment".
- **Credibility is king.** Under-claim and prove it, rather than over-claim. Name sources.
- **Talk about outcomes, not features.** Say what changes for the reader before how it works.
- **Calm, not breathless.** No hype, no exclamation marks, no emoji in professional pieces.
- **Use "you" and "your people".** Not "learners" or "users" in marketing, except in pricing ("per learner").
- **Don't make it about training.** Embed improves performance. Training is the raw material.
